Atlas

66 books, ~1,500 years, ~40 authors, 1 awesome God.

Every arc joins two passages of the Bible that readers have long read together. All 344,799 cross-references are here, across all 31,102 verses from Genesis to Revelation, so you can see the whole web at once and then read any thread of it.

How to explore

  • Tap an arc to read both passages together, then page through every link between those two books.
  • Tap the book strip to read a passage with everything it connects to. Zoom in to go from books to chapters to single verses.
  • Open Galaxy to see every verse as a star. Tap one to light up what it connects to; drag to orbit, and pinch or scroll to zoom. Only the strongest links are drawn until you pick a verse.
  • Search for a passage like John 3:16, Ps 23, or 1 Cor 13:4-7.
  • Press Shuffle for a random connection. On a keyboard, ⌘K or Ctrl K jumps to search, the arrow keys step through verses, chapters, or links, + and − zoom, and Esc clears everything. Controls, at the top right, shows how to explore each view.
  • Every view has its own address, so you can share what you're looking at.

What the votes mean

OpenBible.info readers vote on how helpful each link is. The votes rank the links; they don't prove one right or wrong. By default you see links with at least 4 votes, about 45% of them. Lower the filter to see more, down to every link, including the few readers voted down.

What the colors mean

Each arc fades between the colors of the two sections of the Bible it joins, from Law through Revelation. Colors show where a link sits, not what it means. The source data doesn't sort links by theme.

Sources

Cross-references come from OpenBible.info (2026-10-26 snapshot), drawn mainly from the public-domain Treasury of Scripture Knowledge. They're used under CC BY 4.0 and re-indexed to KJV verse numbering.

Scripture text comes from the Berean Standard Bible, dedicated to the public domain. It leaves out 16 verses that the earliest manuscripts don't have, and the app says so where they appear.

You can also read in the King James Version, World English Bible and American Standard Version (public domain), the Lutherbibel 1912 and Riveduta 1927 (public domain), Bíblia Livre (CC BY 3.0 BR) and Versión Biblia Libre (CC BY-SA 4.0), most from eBible.org. Pick one from the chip at the top of the reading panel.
